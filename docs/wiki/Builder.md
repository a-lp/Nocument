# Builder

![Builder: the document graph of a release note, with the content types and the table of contents](../screenshots/builder.png)

**Documents → Builder** (`/documents/builder`) builds a document starting from a Word document (`.docx`) or template (`.dotx`, opened as a document), uploaded with the button or dropped on the page, from an empty document (**Create empty document**, made from python-docx's default Word template by `/api/builder/new`) or from a saved [template](Templates.md) (**Start from a template** opens the Templates page).

**Open in Compiler** (sidebar) sends the document, as it is now, to the [Compiler](Compiler.md) to fill in its keywords; there, **Open in Builder** brings the document back (the compiled one if it has been compiled, otherwise the original). If the other page already has a document open, the user confirms that it is replaced (`web/src/documentTransfer.js`).

**Save as template** (sidebar, below **Preview PDF**) saves the current document as a [template](Templates.md): a modal asks for its name (required), description and version.

The page has a sidebar and the content graph:

- **Contents** (sidebar, above the table of contents): the same commands as *Add new content* (heading, paragraph, image, table, saved content). They are dragged onto a node of the graph (which is highlighted) to insert them after it, or clicked to add them at the end of the document; the form of the chosen type opens, while **Saved content** opens the choice among the saved contents (as in *Add new content*) and inserts their elements in the document. Styles of the saved contents that the document does not define are replaced with the default ones, with a warning. Contents with headings are inserted as a **block**: if the chosen node is inside a section, they go after the contents and subsections that follow it, up to the next heading of the same or a higher level, so their headings do not take over the rest of that section (e.g. a saved content starting with a Heading 1 dropped on a Heading 1 or on one of its paragraphs goes after that whole section).
- **Table of contents** (sidebar): headings and subheadings only, indented by level and collapsible, like Word's navigation pane. Clicking a heading moves the graph to its node, expanding the sections containing it. At the top **Save PDF**, **Save Word**, **Preview PDF** and **New document**.
- **Content graph**: each node is a content of the document (heading, paragraph, table with caption, image) and each edge means "comes after it in the document, and moves with it". A section is a column on the right of its heading, which branches (dashed edges) into two chains: the **contents** of the section before its first subheading (paragraphs, tables, images), linked in sequence, and the **subheadings**, each with its own section; the heading continues down with the next heading of the same level. The first node, **Beginning of the document**, stands for the beginning of the document. The document's tables of contents (`TOC` fields, paragraphs with the `TOC N`/`TOC Heading` style and table of contents content controls) do not appear: they are regenerated at every update, and the backend rejects changes or insertions inside them.
  - **Exploring**: dragging the background moves the view, the mouse wheel zooms; at the bottom the zoom controls and the minimap (which can be dragged too). The button on a heading's node collapses its section (the node shows how many elements it hides) or expands it; **Expand all** and **Collapse all** at the top right.
  - **Click on a node**: opens the content form, filled in, to edit it (or delete it). Paragraphs with images cannot be edited.
  - **Click and drag from a node**: a line follows the pointer and, on release, a menu appears to add a heading, a paragraph, an image, a table or a saved content **after** that node (after a heading a subheading is suggested; from *Beginning of the document* the content goes to the beginning).
  - **Drag from a node onto another node**: the incoming link of the target node is replaced by one from the source node, i.e. the target moves right after the source (after a heading it becomes the first content of its section; from *Beginning of the document* it goes to the beginning) and the gap it leaves closes. What follows the target in its chain moves with it: a heading moves with its whole section, a content (paragraph, table, image) with the contents that follow it up to the next heading; when more than one element moves it asks for confirmation. A moved heading goes as a block: after the contents and subsections that follow the source, before the next heading of the same or a higher level, so it does not take over the section of the source. While dragging, the node under the pointer turns green (**Move here**), grey (**Already linked**: it already follows the source, nothing happens) or red (**Not allowed**: a heading cannot go inside its own section, nor a content after one of the contents that move with it). Elements containing a Word section break do not move, so the page layout does not change.
  - **Layout tables**: their elements (marked with ⊞ and a dashed border) appear like the others, with headings and sections, and are **edited** and **deleted** one by one like those of the body: headings and paragraphs keep style and properties, nested tables are updated in place; deleting the last paragraph of a cell leaves the cell, empty. **Moving** instead concerns the whole table (after confirmation) and a content added after one of them goes after the table; two elements of the same table are not moved relative to each other. A heading on several lines (e.g. "QUARTERLY" line break "Report") is edited in a text area, which keeps the line breaks.
  - **Right click** (or Shift+F10 from the keyboard): menu with **Edit**, **Select** and **Delete** and the same add items.
  - **Multiple selection**: Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on macOS) or **Select** in the context menu selects or deselects a node. While at least one node is selected, a plain click (or Enter) on a node selects or deselects it instead of opening its editing; the selected nodes are highlighted in the graph and, for headings, in the table of contents of the sidebar; at the top a menu appears with **Select all**, **Deselect all** and **Delete**, which deletes the selected elements together (after confirmation) with a single update of the document.
  - **Keyboard**: Tab goes from a node to the next in document order (the view follows the node), Enter edits it.

**Preview PDF** (sidebar, below the save buttons) opens the PDF of the document in a modal, with the browser's PDF viewer (zoom, search, print) and the **Save PDF** button; it closes with ×, with Esc or by clicking outside. Changes are **lazy**: inserting, editing, moving and deleting only change the Word file (python-docx) and are applied at once. The slow work, updating the tables of contents with LibreOffice and converting to PDF, happens only when the finished document is needed: **Preview PDF**, **Save PDF**, **Save Word** and **Open in Compiler** ask the backend for it (`/api/builder/export`) when clicked, and it is kept until the next change of the document, so a second click does not prepare it again (the buttons are disabled while it is prepared).

The session saves in the browser (IndexedDB) only the last `.docx` returned by the backend: reloading the page reopens the document with `/api/builder/open`, which regenerates its structure, styles and table of contents. **New document** clears the session. If the saved document cannot be reopened, the session is deleted and the upload screen is shown.

The insertion form has four content types, plus the **Saved content** tab that goes to the choice among the saved contents; the styles offered are those of the document, with the template's default one preselected:

- **Heading**: text and heading style (grouped by level; after a heading the next level is suggested, after a content the section's one);
- **Paragraph**: paragraph style and text written with a visual editor (bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, **hyperlinks**, bulleted and numbered lists);
- **Table**: style, alignment, rows and columns, first row as header (bold and repeated on every page), caption above or below. The grid (`TableEditor`, the same in the Compiler, where its columns can also be resized) also imports a CSV and, as in the Compiler, lets you include or exclude columns (also all together), reorder columns and rows and sort by one or more columns by clicking their names; with the header, the column names form the first row and can be edited. The mark in the bottom right corner of each cell (like the resize grip of a text area) opens the **cell editor**, the same visual editor with formatting and hyperlinks; a cell with rich text shows its preview, which opens the editor too. Column names stay plain text.
- **Image**: PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP or TIFF, with a width in cm (by default the original one, within the page) and an alignment (`ImageEditor`, the same in the Compiler without the alignment).

Headings and paragraphs also have an optional direct formatting (font, size, color, alignment; for headings bold, italic and underline): empty fields keep the style's one.

Every insertion is applied by the backend to the `.docx`, which returns the updated document with the new structure. At every change the document's tables of contents are updated (see [PDF renderers](PDF-Renderers.md)): new or edited headings appear with numbering and page both in the preview and in the downloaded Word. New tables take the usable page width; captions use the document's *Caption* style (created with Word's style if the template does not define it). Lists use the *List Bullet*/*List Number* styles if present, otherwise a prefix (`•`, `1.`) with an indent.

When editing, headings and paragraphs keep the original's paragraph properties (indents, spacing, numbering, section breaks) if the style does not change, and the bookmarks used by Word's table of contents links; hyperlinks to web pages and e-mail addresses are kept, while fields and links to places of the document become plain text (the form warns about it).

**Hyperlinks** (`src/builder/rich_text.py`): the **Link** button of the visual editor (or Ctrl+K) asks for the address (`example.org` becomes `https://example.org`, an e-mail address `mailto:`) and, without selected text, the text to show; on a link it also offers **Remove link**. Addresses typed or pasted in the editor become links by themselves. In the document a link is a real Word hyperlink with the *Hyperlink* character style (created with Word's look if the template does not define it). Only web, e-mail and FTP addresses are accepted. Table cells are plain text or, when edited with formatting or links, `{"html": "..."}` (in `rows` of the table contents, of `/api/builder/*` and of the `TableCompiler`); cells read from a document are rich only if they contain links. Tables are updated in place, keeping the cell borders, colors and widths; added rows copy the last one. Only changing the number of columns recreates the table. Paragraphs containing images can only be deleted. Deleting a heading moves the contents of its section to the previous section.

Limits: the caption is plain text, without Word's automatic numbering.
